# Form-answer rules: government employment vs. supporting military / defense missions (JSA-316)

The rules answer job-application questions for one candidate from his facts. Each question has a label, optional options
(the list the form offers) and the hiring company. The answer is one option text (exactly as offered), a text (for a box
with no options), or "unknown" (the rules do not answer: the candidate is asked).

## The candidate's facts
- Student (F-1, citizen of China). Has NEVER been employed by any government (federal, state, local, foreign), never
  in civil service, never in any military, never held or sought public office, never a public official. No family
  member works or worked for a government or holds public office.
- He has ONE saved answer about defense work, for exactly this kind of question: "Are there any reasons why you would
  not be able to fully contribute in support of the U.S. military and allied forces?" → Yes (there may be reasons:
  he is a foreign national and export control may apply). The saved answer covers questions that ask whether there is
  any reason he would / could not (be able to) (fully) contribute to / support / work for military, defense, armed
  forces or allied-forces missions.

## What the rules must do
1. A question about the candidate's own (or his family's) government, civil-service or military employment, service,
   office or official position ("Are you a current or former government employee?", "Have you ever been employed by a
   local, state, or federal government entity, including the U.S. military?", "Do you hold or are you seeking an
   elected or appointed government position?", "Have you served in the military?", "Is any family member employed by a
   government agency?") is answered No.
2. A question about his willingness, ability or any reason not to support or contribute to military / defense missions,
   defense projects, defense customers or the armed forces is NEVER answered No by a rule — even when it also names the
   U.S. military. If it is the saved kind (any reason he would/could not contribute to / support the military / defense /
   armed forces / allied forces) the answer is the saved one: Yes. Every other such question (e.g. "Are you willing to
   work on projects that support the Department of Defense?", "Are you comfortable working on military applications?")
   is "unknown" (the candidate is asked).
3. A company description that mentions the military before a question about his own government employment does not
   change rule 1 when the question itself is only about his employment.
Answers are exact option texts when options are given; for Yes / No options, "Yes" or "No".
