# Eligibility: soft blocks that must not kill a job (JSA-231, 2026-11-06)

The gate classifies a job description as Eligible / Review / Ineligible for one candidate: an F-1 M.S. student (not a
citizen or permanent resident; needs visa sponsorship for full-time work later; a Summer 2027 internship runs on CPT),
graduating December 2027, U.S. jobs only. "Review" means a person reads the posting before applying.

1. Experience (full-time roles only). A REQUIRED minimum of 3 or more years of experience → Ineligible; a required 2 years
   → Review. A minimum that is only a preference → Review, never Ineligible: the line sits under a heading such as
   "Preferred Qualifications", "Desired background", "Nice to have", "Bonus", "Ideally, you'd have", or the line itself
   ends with "preferred" / "is a plus". When the posting has a labelled "Minimum Years of Work Experience: 0", a 3+ years
   line elsewhere is a preference. "Up to 3 years of experience" (also "no more than", "less than") is a ceiling: it never
   blocks. A required floor wins over a preferred one (Required: 4+ years, Preferred: 6+ years → Ineligible). Headings
   such as "Minimum / Basic / Required Qualifications", "You might be a good fit if you have", "Our ideal engineer will
   have" introduce requirements, not preferences; "ideally in a startup" after a requirement does not soften it.
2. MBA. A title that says MBA blocks only when it names no other degree: "Product Management Intern (Master's/MBA)" and
   "(MS or MBA)" do not block by the title; "MBA Product Intern" does.
3. Internship length. More than about 4 months blocks ("Duration: 6 months", "minimum 6-month commitment",
   "lasts 5-6 months"). A range that starts at 4 months or less fits a summer and does not block ("3-6 month internship",
   "4 to 8-month commitment").
4. Class year (internships, for this master's candidate). "Rising juniors and seniors", "rising sophomore or junior",
   "juniors or seniors" without a master's / graduate mention → Ineligible. When the same phrase is followed by
   "preferred" / "is a plus" ("Rising Junior or Senior preferred", "junior or senior standing preferred") → Review.
5. Graduation window (candidate graduates December 2027). A window that excludes December 2027 → Ineligible (e.g. "must
   be graduating August 2027 or prior"). An extra window introduced with "may also be considered" / "are also welcome"
   only widens the main one ("graduating between August 2027 and June 2028; students graduating in 2029 may also be
   considered" → Eligible). Two separate windows in the posting, one that includes December 2027 and one that does not
   → Review.
6. Sponsorship. "We do not sponsor visas", "unable to sponsor", "sponsorship is not available" → Ineligible. A sentence
   that only says the company does not participate in / support the STEM OPT extension (and says nothing about
   sponsorship, H-1B, F-1, CPT, OPT or visas) → Review, not Ineligible. A no-sponsorship sentence plus discretionary
   wording near sponsorship ("at its sole discretion ... whether to offer sponsorship", "case-by-case", "may be
   considered in limited circumstances") → Review. A sentence that combines STEM OPT with "we do not sponsor visas"
   → Ineligible.
Everything else in the cases is neutral: a U.S. city, a Summer 2027 internship (or a full-time role starting 2028) and a
statement that master's students are welcome — except where the case tests that line.
