# Work-authorization answers for an F-1 student (JSA-310, decided by the candidate 2026-11-11)

Candidate: F-1 international student (Master's, University of Chicago, through December 2027). The Summer 2027 internship
will be on CPT; full-time from 2028 on OPT, then H-1B. Citizen of China only; not a U.S. citizen, not a permanent resident,
no green card. He needs visa sponsorship in the future (for H-1B); sponsorship "now" for the internship alone = No.

Rules the form-answer engine must follow:
1. Every work-AUTHORIZATION question is answered Yes — including "Are you legally authorized to work for any U.S.
   employer?", "authorized to work for all employers", "authorized to work without restriction / unrestricted",
   "authorized to work as of today / effective as of the date you complete this application", "legally entitled /
   eligible / permitted to work in the U.S.".
2. When the options are statements, the engine picks the unrestricted authorized statement, e.g. "Yes, I am authorized
   to work in this country for any employer", "I am legally authorized to work for any employer in the United States",
   "Yes, without restrictions". A "Yes, with restrictions" / "authorized for my present employer only" / "I require
   sponsorship" statement is picked only when no plain or unrestricted Yes statement is offered. A statement that he is
   NOT authorized is never picked.
3. A Yes option that claims U.S. citizenship, permanent residence, a green card, permanent authorization, or that he does
   not need / will not need sponsorship is never picked.
4. "Are you PERMANENTLY authorized to work (for any employer) in the U.S.?" → No.
5. Sponsorship questions do not change: "Will you now or in the future require sponsorship?" → Yes; statement options →
   the one saying he will require sponsorship. "Are you authorized to work … WITHOUT (current or future) sponsorship?"
   with no limit to the internship → No; the same limited to "this internship / now / currently" → Yes.
   "…for any employer without the current or future need for sponsorship?" is a sponsorship question → No.
6. "Are you a U.S. citizen or permanent resident?" → No. "What is your current immigration / visa status?" → F-1
   (the option "F-1" when offered).
