# Work-authorization answers for an F-1 / CPT student (JSA-291)

The rules answer job-application questions for one candidate from his facts. Each question has a label and options (a
list the form offers; an empty list = a text box). The answer is one option text exactly as offered, a text, or
"unknown" (the rules do not answer: the candidate is asked).

## The candidate's facts
- F-1 student in the United States. He holds work authorization only through CPT, which is tied to one specific employer:
  - "Are you legally authorized to work in the U.S.?" (plain, no "any employer" / "without restriction") = Yes.
  - "Are you authorized to work for ANY employer / without restriction / permanently / unrestricted?" = No.
  - Requires visa sponsorship now = No (for the internship, CPT). Will require sponsorship in the future = Yes.
  - He is not a U.S. citizen or permanent resident.

## The rule under test
1. A yes/no question (options exactly "Yes" / "No", or a text box) asking whether he is authorized for ANY employer /
   without restriction → "No".
2. A STATUS LIST — three or more statements describing work-authorization statuses, at least one of which mentions
   "any employer" / "without restriction" / "unrestricted" or says he is "not authorized" — is not a yes/no question:
   a. If an option starts with "Yes, with restrictions" (or "Yes, but with restrictions") → that option.
   b. Otherwise, the option stating that he requires / needs (now or in the future) visa sponsorship → that option. An
      option saying he does NOT require sponsorship is never this one.
   c. Never pick an option saying he is "not authorized" / "not currently authorized" / "not eligible" to work, nor the
      option claiming authorization for any employer / without restriction / permanently / as a citizen.
   d. If neither (a) nor (b) is offered → "unknown" (he is asked). Options such as "for my present employer only" or
      "my status is unknown" are not picked by the rule.
3. Questions that are not about work authorization are out of scope; do not write cases for them.
